# Twenty-video multiple-choice native-AV, fusion, and evidence-agent diagnostic


Date: 2026-08-21

Evidence validity: **Verified for completed-run contract validity and
frozen-panel scoring; verified preflight only for the four-option agent;
diagnostic for model promotion and routing**

Decision disposition: **Not promoted under the tested condition for
blind fusion, a Qwen submission, one-shot adaptive inspection, or
deterministic single-model deliberation; the four-option agent was not
launched under its authorized cost contract; Gemini remains the
provisional full-panel control**

## Completed evidence record

- **Primary theme:** routing, fusion, and answer selection.
- **Related themes:** models and adaptation, visual and temporal
  evidence, and speech/transcripts.
- **Question:** on a small stratified multiple-choice panel, do direct
  Gemini native audio-video, Qwen native video, Qwen video plus
  transcript, or an equal-family Borda fusion provide a safe path to
  scale? On the same frozen panel, can a second question-selected
  native-AV inspection improve Gemini? Can a later bounded single-model
  deliberative loop avoid the adaptive prototype’s regressions?
- **Hypothesis:** independent model families and evidence views may make
  complementary errors, allowing a family-balanced fusion to outperform
  either family alone. For the adaptive follow-up, a question-selected
  native-AV interval at model-chosen temporal and spatial detail may
  resolve a close top-two decision that the complete low-resolution pass
  missed. For the deliberative follow-up, competing hypotheses, an
  append-only evidence notebook, up to two inspections, and a fresh
  judge may prevent premature commitment.
- **Falsifier:** contract failures, a budget breach, or no fusion
  improvement over the strongest single path; for the adaptive
  follow-up, no final improvement over both its first stage and the
  frozen Gemini control, or repairs that do not exceed regressions; for
  the deliberative follow-up, no improvement over 15/20, repairs that do
  not exceed regressions, repeated prior regressions, nonadaptive paths,
  invalid finals, incomplete integrity, or a cost breach.
- **Canonical claim:** this report and [the routing-theme
  record](../project-status/research-routing.md#mc-poc20).
- **Sanitized evidence bundle:**
  [`artifacts/vqa-mc-poc20-20260821/`](artifacts/vqa-mc-poc20-20260821/README.md).

Questions, answer choices, transcripts, labels, row-level predictions,

## Tested condition and panel

The panel contained 20 locally available VQA-2025 training videos, with
four rows each from temporal, causal, OCR/detail, counting, and speech
strata. Five previously inspected canaries were retained; the other rows
were selected from short, transcript-available examples using label-free
metadata. Correct choice positions were balanced at five each for A, B,
C, and D. Expected answers were isolated from both inference runners
until predictions froze.

Gemini 3.7 Flash received complete native video and audio. Qwen3.6-27B
at revision `6a9e13bd6fc8f0983b9b99948120bc37f49c13e9` received two
matched views: native video and video plus the existing Whisper
transcript. Qwen ran in the digest-pinned vLLM runtime
`sha256:77797441eae630c2e79eefa03957b3d61a278670f2a9928d64ce102e7a0790cc`.
Every valid output had to provide a strict ranking of all four choices.
The within-Qwen result and the equal-family Gemini/Qwen result used
Borda scores; the two Qwen calls could not outvote Gemini merely by call
count.

The initial Gemini attempt used an incorrect 128-token cap with high
thinking: all 27 responses exhausted their budget before the required
JSON ranking. Its bounded recovery restored the tested 2,048-token cap
and reran only the 20 canonical rows. The initial Qwen task failed
before model startup because its private media prefix was empty. A
repaired task used a verified 20-file private media prefix without
changing the scientific inputs.

## Two-stage adaptive-inspection follow-up

The follow-up kept the same frozen 20 rows and original Gemini
predictions as the control. Labels remained isolated until all new
predictions froze. Gemini 3.7 Flash first received each complete native
audio-video clip at 4 FPS and low media resolution. It produced a
four-choice ranking plus one unrestricted inspection request: a time
interval, 1/4/8 FPS, low/high resolution, an evidence-gap category, and
a question for the second pass. The second call received that native-AV
interval and adjudicated the first pass’s top two. An invalid second
response fell back to the frozen valid first-stage ranking.

| Condition                    |   Valid |         Top-1 |        MRR |
|------------------------------|--------:|--------------:|-----------:|
| Frozen direct Gemini control | 20 / 20 | 15 / 20 (75%) | **0.8667** |
| Adaptive stage 1             | 20 / 20 | 15 / 20 (75%) |     0.8542 |
| Adaptive final               | 20 / 20 | 15 / 20 (75%) |     0.8583 |

The adaptive final repaired one first-stage error and regressed one
first-stage-correct answer. Relative to the frozen direct control, it
repaired two and regressed two. Nineteen second-stage responses were
valid; the one invalid response used the predeclared first-stage
fallback, leaving 20 valid final rankings. The run made 40 calls, used
116,098 prompt tokens and 28,645 output tokens, and cost `$0.09724613`,
below its `$0.75` cap.

The predeclared gate failed: the final result exceeded neither stage 1
nor the frozen control, and repairs did not exceed regressions. This is
a valid negative result, so **one-shot adaptive inspection is not
promoted under the tested condition**.

### Why the adaptive pass did not help

The agent requested a legal inspection for every row, but its behavior
was systematically narrow:

- no requested interval covered more than 75% of its clip;
- 13/20 inspections remained at low resolution;
- 11/20 used 4 FPS, 5/20 used 1 FPS, and only 4/20 used 8 FPS; and
- a single local view could not revisit the complete clip, compare
  competing windows, or recover when its top-two hypothesis was wrong.

The second pass therefore moved errors instead of reducing them. It did
repair one rapid-action/counting miss with a short 8-FPS/high-resolution
interval, but another counting inspection changed a correct first-stage
answer to a wrong one. The lone apparent speech gain over the frozen
control came from the new first-stage planning prompt and survived
through fallback after invalid second-stage JSON; it was not evidence
that the focused second call resolved the speech question.

This follow-up does not show that adaptive evidence collection is
ineffective. It rules out this one-window, one-reconsideration topology
as sufficient on the selected panel. A richer bounded agent should
preserve a global evidence ledger, form competing top-two hypotheses,
request up to two distinct local inspections, backtrack when evidence
conflicts or fails to distinguish the choices, and let a final arbiter
see both global and local evidence.

## Bounded deliberative-agent follow-up

The next follow-up tested that richer topology with one Gemini model.
Two complete-video solvers produced option-by-option hypotheses and
observations; a text planner could stop or request up to two blinded
native-AV views at model-selected intervals, FPS, and resolution; an
append-only notebook retained all evidence; and a fresh complete-video
judge made the final ranking. Labels remained isolated until the final
predictions and notebook hashes froze.

The predeclared falsifier required more than 15/20, more repairs than
regressions, preservation of both earlier adaptive-regression hits, 20
valid finals, complete structural integrity, at least one
zero-inspection path, at least two distinct action configurations, and
cost at or below `$0.75`.

Two bounded host-contract corrections preceded the completed result. The
`a1` attempt was invalid because the configured output ceiling included
hidden high-thinking tokens; all initial solver JSON records failed and
the intended agent never ran. The `a2` correction was stopped after 27
solver calls when the host parser rejected complete option-keyed arrays
merely because Gemini returned them in ranking order rather than A–D
order. The completed `a3` run mechanically normalized unique
option-keyed rows and reused all 27 immutable `a2` solver responses
without resampling. These corrections changed transport and parsing
only, not prompts, labels, semantic decisions, metrics, or gates.

| Condition                     |   Valid |             Top-1 |        MRR |
|-------------------------------|--------:|------------------:|-----------:|
| Frozen direct Gemini control  | 20 / 20 | **15 / 20 (75%)** | **0.8667** |
| Prior two-call adaptive final | 20 / 20 | **15 / 20 (75%)** |     0.8583 |
| Deliberative anchor           | 19 / 20 |     14 / 20 (70%) |     0.8125 |
| Deliberative blind solver     | 20 / 20 |     14 / 20 (70%) |     0.8292 |
| Last valid planner            | 18 / 20 |     14 / 20 (70%) |     0.7875 |
| Deliberative final judge      | 20 / 20 | **14 / 20 (70%)** |     0.8292 |

Relative to the frozen control, the final repaired **0**, regressed
**1**, kept 14 correct, and kept five wrong. It also repeated one of the
two previously observed adaptive-agent regressions. Temporal and causal
remained 4/4 each; OCR/detail, counting, and speech were 2/4 each.

### Why the richer topology did not activate

This was not a failure of high-resolution evidence after it was
requested. No observer view was requested at all:

- among 19 rows with two valid solver records, the solvers agreed on
  top-1 in **19/19** and on the full ranking in 15/19;
- all 18 valid planner decisions chose `finish_search`; four correction
  calls recovered two initially invalid planner records, while two
  remained invalid;
- zero rows used one or two observer actions, so there were no distinct
  media action configurations; and
- the final judge matched the blind solver’s top choice in **20/20**
  rows and changed the frozen control’s top choice only once, which was
  a regression.

The intended “independent” solvers used the same model, complete-video
prompt, seed, and media settings. Their unanimous top choices therefore
provided duplicated confidence rather than useful epistemic diversity.
The planner then treated agreement as settled even when solver records
contained unresolved questions or proposed checks. The judge received
the resulting consensus-heavy notebook and ratified it. This is a
**confirmed design defect** in the tested deliberation protocol: it
formed an echo chamber instead of an adversarial evidence loop.

Post-label native-media review covered all six final misses. None had
all four options wrong. Four reference answers were clearly supported:
one exact misspelling was silently normalized, one complete gesture
trajectory was reduced to a local pose, one visible reception was
redefined using a narrower sport-outcome meaning, and one flexing pose
was merged into a transition. Two other rows had a defensible second
option: one visual-description question and one
dialogue-versus-broad-cause choice. These manual findings diagnose the
selected rows; official scoring remains unchanged.

The completed `a3` path made 84 semantic calls: 27 reused solver calls
and 57 new provider calls. Across invalid `a1`, interrupted `a2`, and
completed `a3`, observed cost was `$0.26213850`; the final combined
preflight ceiling was `$0.66699825`, below the authorized `$0.75`. All
20 final notebooks, declared source hashes, provenance records, and
citations verified.

The result is **valid but negative** and the protocol is **not promoted
under the tested condition**. More FPS or resolution alone cannot help
when the controller never opens an evidence view. A next agent should
create actual diversity—different solver roles or seeds, with one
explicitly searching for a counterexample—and require an adversarial
stop review that may open evidence when reported uncertainty or
candidate checks remain. Because this panel is now heavily inspected,
any promotion claim requires a fresh label-isolated panel; the current
20 rows are only a regression and mechanism suite.

## Four-option evidence-agent preflight

The next protocol removed consensus stopping. It preserved the frozen
direct Gemini ranking as a protected fallback, kept A–D in an explicit
evidence ledger, required at least one blinded native-AV observation,
allowed up to three planner/observer/updater/critic rounds, and required
new cited observer evidence before the final judge could replace the
protected answer. All roles used fresh contexts of the same Gemini
model; no training or Qwen call was included.

The declared execution boundary was a five-row canary made from the
frozen control’s five misses, followed automatically by the 20-row
regression panel only if the canary repaired at least one miss and
passed every structural gate. The model was to receive high thinking and
its advertised maximum output allowance. The combined formal worst-case
cost—including one recovery for every possible semantic call—had to
remain at or below the authorized `$1.50` before any paid generation.

One input-only correction preceded the valid preflight. The first
private canary payload contained no answers but named its label-file
hash `labels`; the inference loader rejected that forbidden
label-bearing field name. The versioned correction renamed it to neutral
selection provenance and passed label isolation. Neither preparation
made a provider generation call.

Live Gemini metadata reported model version `3.7-flash-08-2026`, a
1,048,576-token input limit, and a 65,536-token output maximum. Pricing
every permitted recovery at that output maximum produced this preflight:

| Boundary                            |              Formal ceiling |
|-------------------------------------|----------------------------:|
| Rows / video duration               |          20 / 246.9 seconds |
| Semantic calls                      |                         280 |
| Provider calls including recoveries |                         560 |
| Input tokens / cost                 |   3,036,937 / `$1.13885137` |
| Output tokens / cost                | 36,700,160 / `$68.81280000` |
| Combined cost                       |          **`$69.95165137`** |
| Authorized cap                      |                 **`$1.50`** |

The formal ceiling exceeded authorization, so the host blocked the run
before media upload. **Zero videos were uploaded, zero generative calls
ran, and no predictions or accuracy metrics exist for this protocol.**
This is a verified preflight result, not evidence that the architecture
helps or fails on MC.

The immediate decision is to preserve the implementation and choose a
new cost contract before inference. Keeping the provider-maximum output
allowance while demanding that all 560 hypothetical calls consume their
maximum is incompatible with `$1.50`. A later launch therefore requires
either a much higher formal authorization or an explicitly approved
empirical/dynamic reservation policy; silently imposing smaller
reasoning-token caps would change the user-approved treatment.

## Failure diagnosis and repair hypotheses

Gemini placed the correct answer second on four of its five misses and
third on the remaining miss. Correct positions were balanced, while
misses spanned multiple letters; permutation bias is therefore not the
leading explanation. The five errors divide into one exact-spelling OCR
normalization error, two event-counting failures, and two
speech/visual-detail selections with close or incomplete evidence.

- **OCR:** use a high-resolution question-conditioned crop and
  explicitly preserve misspellings instead of normalizing visible text.
- **Counting:** reuse the existing full-video AV event map to propose
  several relevant windows, then run a dense event-boundary counter
  across those windows. The prior event map resolves one observed miss
  but not the other, so it is routed evidence rather than a universal
  answerer.
- **Speech and close alternatives:** retain audio through the final
  utterance, align the relevant ASR span, and adjudicate the top two
  choices against cited evidence. Qwen transcript corrected one Gemini
  miss but introduced three errors on Gemini-correct rows, so it should
  not override Gemini globally.
- **Answer election:** because four correct answers were already ranked
  second, a small evidence-citing top-two verifier is more directly
  targeted than permutation consensus or unrestricted voting.

A post-hoc rule that used Qwen transcript only on this panel’s four
speech rows scored 16/20 (80%). This rule was discovered after scoring
and is not a valid promotion result. It is only a hypothesis for a
fresh, label-blind confirmatory panel.

## What this rules out now

- Do not treat native-video Qwen, within-Qwen Borda, or equal-family
  Borda as a third submission merely because the model families are
  independent.
- Do not use Qwen-view agreement as a confidence gate: the views can
  agree on the same wrong choice.
- Do not make permutation-debiasing the next priority; the observed
  errors do not show a dominant choice-position mechanism.
- Do not replace the strongest direct path with the older event-map
  approach; reuse its selected evidence only where it addresses a
  measured failure.
- Do not promote a single question-selected crop merely because it uses
  native video and can request higher FPS or resolution; the tested pass
  stayed at 15/20 and introduced one stage-1 regression.
- Do not claim that the mandatory-inspection four-option agent was
  tested for answer quality: its strict provider-maximum cost
  reservation blocked every paid call before media upload.

## What it does not rule out

This 20-row selected panel does not choose the final official
submission. It does not rule out Qwen on a representative full panel, a
predeclared speech route, a stronger OCR/count specialist, or
evidence-aware top-two election. It also does not estimate accuracy on
VQA-2026 evaluation videos. The adaptive follow-up does not rule out a
bounded multi-inspection agent that retains global context, backtracks,
and arbitrates explicit competing evidence. The deliberative follow-up
rules out duplicated deterministic solvers plus an unconstrained stop
decision as sufficient; because no observer action ran, it does not
measure whether two well-chosen inspections would help. The later
four-option preflight likewise does not measure the mandatory-inspection
architecture; it only establishes that the approved `$1.50` cap is
incompatible with reserving the provider maximum for every permitted
call and recovery.

## Next decision test

Keep Gemini native AV as the unchanged primary/control path. On a fresh,
label-blind confirmatory panel, compare it with:

1.  Qwen video plus transcript only for a predeclared speech route, with
    abstention to Gemini;
2.  high-resolution OCR crops with exact-spelling instructions;
3.  top-k AV event-map windows plus a dense counting pass; and
4.  a bounded evidence agent with a full-video A–D ledger, mandatory
    blinded observation, adversarial stop review, up to three
    inspections, and a final arbiter with an explicit retain-the-control
    option when evidence is weak or conflicting—but only after
    predeclaring a cost policy that can actually authorize the run.

Advance a route only if it improves paired accuracy over Gemini without
regressing non-target controls. A valid final submission choice still
requires the intended larger panel; this diagnostic alone is not a
promotion benchmark.
